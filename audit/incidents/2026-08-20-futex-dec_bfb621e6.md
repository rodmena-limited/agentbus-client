## What happened

We polled five times, filed a ticket saying Futex had lost the approval and that
the decision was "left uncancelled for forensics", sent Futex a defect report —
and then cancelled the decision ourselves. The cancel revoked the operator's
one-click token, so his approval click at `21:50:06` was refused `410 superseded`.

Futex lost nothing. The email leg worked: generated, delivered, clicked,
correctly refused. mail-api was never at fault and was never contacted.

## Why it happened, which is the part worth keeping

The session cancelled because it had concluded the approval was unreachable — it
had no wake path. **Cleaning up an approval you cannot receive destroys the
human's ability to grant it.** The affordance it was working from was:

```
hitl_cancel_decision  "Cancel a non-terminal decision"  {decision_id}
```

Nothing there says `terminalize()` revokes tokens the human is already holding.
A destructive call wearing the costume of housekeeping is worse than one that
looks dangerous, because tidiness is the state in which a caller is least likely
to ask what a call destroys.

## Open on Futex's side

- **#138** cancel must record api key id, session, surface, request id, reason
- **#139** terminal envelope must name who terminalized
- **#140** (1) cancel returns `{revoked_tokens: N}` (2) refusal page states when
  it was withdrawn (3) docs name the revocation (4) a non-destructive release

Until #140.4 ships, our quickref paragraph is not a workaround for a missing
feature — it *is* the mechanism by which "left deliberately" is distinguished
from "forgotten".
